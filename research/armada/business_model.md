**Phase 3F** · Prepared 2026-08-21.

## Current observed model

**OBSERVED.** Every dollar ARMADA is publicly known to have received is a **competitively awarded federal development instrument**:

| Instrument | Count | Value |
|---|---:|---:|
| SBIR/STTR Phase I (NSF, Navy, NOAA, OSD) | 4 | $826,961 |
| Navy SBIR Phase II (base $999,028 + exercised option $499,949 + CLIN 0004 $499,949) | 1 | $1,998,926 |

| **Total verified federal** | | **$2,972,287** |

**OBSERVED.** No commercial sale, licence income, pilot agreement or named commercial customer appears anywhere in the public record reviewed.

> **Stated plainly: this is not revenue.** A Phase II contract is the government paying for development it wants done. It is evidence of funded technical demand and of a customer sufficiently satisfied to exercise an option — it is **not** product-market fit, and treating SBIR obligations as revenue would misrepresent the company.

**INFERRED — the current model is contract R&D.** ARMADA today is a three-person engineering organisation funded by sequential government development contracts. That is a legitimate and often sensible way to fund deep-tech development, and the founders have executed it well: four agencies, five years, an exercised option and fresh incremental funding in March 2026.

## Plausible scaled models

Five models are available to a company in this position. They are not equally attractive.

### A. Subsystem sale to UUV OEMs — *the venture case*
Sell propulsion modules, or EPADS pods, as a per-vehicle or per-unit component.
- **Attractive because:** recurring, gross-margin-bearing, capital-light, scales with someone else's manufacturing.
- **Hard because:** requires an OEM design-in, which invalidates existing qualification and takes years; buyer concentration is extreme; and the OEM is the party most able to build it instead.
- **Evidence today:** none. No OEM relationship is public.

### B. Consumable payload pods — *the most interesting and least examined*
**INFERRED.** EPADS pods are **left on the seafloor with the payload**. If correct, every deployment consumes a pod, which converts a subsystem into a **consumable with recurring unit volume** — structurally the most attractive economics available to ARMADA, and far better than a durable propulsion module sold once per vehicle.
- **Evidence today:** the mechanism is described in the SBIR abstract and the patent claim; the *business* implication is our inference and is unconfirmed.
- **This should be the first commercial question asked of the founders.**

### C. Licensing
License Asymmetric Propulsion to OEMs rather than manufacturing.
- **Attractive because:** capital-light; suits a three-person team; ARMADA already operates inside a licensing relationship with WHOI, so the mechanics are familiar.
- **Hard because:** royalty economics rarely support venture-scale outcomes at these unit volumes; and ARMADA is a *licensee* of the underlying patents — its ability to sublicense is **unknown** and is a real constraint.

### D. Complete vehicle / system sale
Move up the stack and sell platforms.
- **Attractive because:** captures the largest observed procurement bucket ($60.3M platform purchases in the sample).
- **Hard because:** it is a fundamentally different, far more capital-intensive company, competing directly with HII, Teledyne, Kongsberg and Saab — and, within Propeller's own disclosed portfolio, with VATN and Orpheus. It also abandons the capital-light subsystem logic that makes ARMADA interesting.

### E. Continued contract R&D — *the default*
Keep winning SBIR/BAA work.
- **Attractive because:** non-dilutive, proven, and the largest observed bucket in the procurement sample ($69.0M R&D).
- **Hard because:** it does not compound. Revenue is won bespokely, headcount scales with contracts, and the outcome is a good engineering business — **not a venture return.** This is the gravitational default the company must escape, and the March 2026 CLIN funding shows the pull is real and ongoing.

## The central venture question

> **Can ARMADA evolve from grant- and contract-funded technical development into a repeatable commercial business?**

**What supports "yes":**
- The EPADS consumable-pod hypothesis (model B) would give genuinely recurring unit economics, and the technology is furthest along.
- Federal R&D funding has de-risked the technology without dilution — $3.0M of development for no equity is a real advantage at seed.
- A Navy Phase II with an exercised option and 2026 incremental funding is the strongest possible foundation for a Phase III transition, which is the intended commercialisation bridge.

**What supports "no", or "not yet":**
- Five years in, **zero commercial revenue, zero named commercial customer, zero public OEM relationship**.
- The directly addressable procurement observed is **under $1M/year** (see `procurement_market.md`).
- The team is three people, and the model that scales (subsystem sales) requires manufacturing, qualification and enterprise sales capabilities none of them publicly demonstrates.
- The company's marketed product (propulsion) has the weakest evidence; its best-evidenced product (EPADS) is single-customer.

**INFERRED conclusion.** ARMADA is currently model E with an option on models A and B. The investable question is not whether the technology works — the evidence there is respectable — but whether a transition to B has begun. **Public information cannot answer that.** It is the first thing primary research must establish.
