# ARMADA — Commercial Diligence

**Phase 3C** · Prepared 2026-08-21.

**Framing rule applied throughout:** *potential* customer ≠ *demonstrated* customer. ARMADA has **no publicly evidenced commercial sale to any customer**. Every segment below is a hypothesis about where demand could come from, assessed on public evidence about the segment — not on any ARMADA relationship, because none is public. **No customer conversations were conducted and none are invented.**

Analysis is by product line, because the two lines have different buyers.

---

## Segment 1 — US Navy / DoD (EPADS primary; propulsion secondary)

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Job to be done** | Deliver a payload to a specified seafloor location from an uncrewed vehicle, without a crewed vessel, diver, or vehicle modification. |
| **What they use today** | Internally-carried payloads requiring vehicle modification, or crewed/diver emplacement. **OBSERVED:** the Navy wrote an SBIR topic for this, which is itself evidence the incumbent options were judged inadequate. |
| **Pain with the incumbent** | Internal carriage consumes vehicle volume and requires per-vehicle engineering; diver emplacement is dangerous and shallow-limited. |
| **Budget owner** | Navy programme offices; the contracting activity here is N68335 (NAVAIR/NAWCAD Lakehurst). SBIR Phase III or a programme of record would be the transition path. |
| **Proof required before adoption** | Placement accuracy characterisation (the stated Phase II objective), depth qualification, safety and acoustic release reliability, and integration approval on a fielded host vehicle. |
| **What is ARMADA selling?** | **Subsystem / attachable module.** Not a platform, not software. |
| **Recurring or one-time?** | **INFERRED:** potentially recurring if payload pods are consumable — the pod is *left on the seafloor with the payload*, which would make each deployment a unit sale. This is the single most attractive economic feature of EPADS and deserves direct confirmation. |
| **Sales cycle risk** | Very long. SBIR Phase II → Phase III transition is the classic "valley of death"; many Phase IIs never transition. |
| **Certification** | Yes — any hardware attached to a fielded Navy UUV requires qualification and safety review. |
| **Buyer concentration** | Extreme. Effectively one customer with several programme offices. |

**Demonstrated?** ARMADA has a funded Navy development contract with an exercised option and incremental funding as recently as March 2026. **That is funded technical demand and a satisfied contracting officer. It is not a product sale and not a programme of record.**

## Segment 2 — UUV OEMs (propulsion primary)

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Job to be done** | Deliver thrust and control with fewer actuators, lower drag and higher reliability; and add low-speed inspection capability to a survey vehicle. |
| **What they use today** | Conventional thruster plus 2–4 control fins with servo actuators. Mature, qualified, well-understood. |
| **Pain with the incumbent** | Each actuator is a pressure penetration and a failure mode; fins add drag; survey-optimised vehicles hover poorly. Real pain, but **tolerable** — the industry has shipped this architecture for decades. |
| **Budget owner** | OEM engineering/product organisation, not a procurement budget. This is a *design decision*, not a purchase. |
| **Proof required** | Reliability data over full deployment cycles; efficiency and control-authority data versus their existing baseline; acoustic characterisation; and a credible supply guarantee from a three-person company. |
| **What is ARMADA selling?** | A propulsion **subsystem**, or possibly a **licence**. |
| **Recurring?** | Yes if designed in — subsystem revenue per vehicle built. This is the attractive model. |
| **Sales cycle risk** | **The critical barrier.** Changing propulsion architecture invalidates a vehicle's qualification and touches hull design, autonomy and control software. Realistic design-in cycles are multi-year, and OEMs are conservative because their customers are conservative. |
| **Certification** | Indirect but decisive — via the OEM's own qualification. |
| **Buyer concentration** | High. Teledyne, Kongsberg, Saab, HII, L3Harris and a modest tail of smaller builders. |

**Demonstrated?** **No public evidence of any OEM relationship, pilot, or design-in.** This is the largest commercial gap in the case.

## Segment 3 — Oceanographic research institutions

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Job to be done** | Deploy and recover instruments; survey; increasingly, do both without a research vessel. |
| **What they use today** | REMUS-class AUVs, gliders, moorings, ROVs — largely from Teledyne/Kongsberg/HII, or built in-house. **OBSERVED:** the procurement sample shows NOAA buying a Saab long-range AUV ($1.68M) and two UW Seagliders ($500K), and WHOI selling the Navy a REMUS 600 ($1.99M). |
| **Pain with the incumbent** | Ship time is the dominant cost of any at-sea operation. |
| **Budget owner** | Federal science budgets (NSF, NOAA, ONR) and institutional funds. |
| **Proof required** | Field reliability; peer credibility. This segment is unusually receptive to novel technology from known researchers. |
| **What is ARMADA selling?** | Subsystem, or potentially complete small platforms. |
| **Recurring?** | Weakly. Small unit volumes, long asset lives. |
| **Sales cycle risk** | Grant-cycle timing; small budgets. |
| **Buyer concentration** | Moderate; a few dozen institutions globally. |
| **Structural problem** | **INFERRED:** this segment is where ARMADA's credibility is highest and its revenue potential is lowest. It is a reference-customer market, not a scale market. And **ARMADA's own origin institution is a competitor here** — WHOI builds and sells vehicles. |

## Segment 4 — Offshore energy, subsea infrastructure and survey contractors

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Job to be done** | Inspect subsea cables, pipelines, moorings and structures; place and recover sensors. |
| **What they use today** | Work-class ROVs from a support vessel, and increasingly resident/hybrid AUVs. |
| **Pain with the incumbent** | Vessel day rates are the dominant cost — this is exactly the pain the "one asset instead of two" argument targets, and it is worth far more here than the price of a propulsion module. |
| **Budget owner** | Operators (offshore wind developers, cable owners) and their survey contractors. |
| **Proof required** | Demonstrated inspection-grade station-keeping in current; commercial reliability; and typically a track record the company does not yet have. |
| **What is ARMADA selling?** | Subsystem into a vehicle builder, or capability into a survey contractor. |
| **Recurring?** | Potentially strong if inspection becomes a routine service. |
| **Sales cycle risk** | Conservative buyers; safety-critical assets; procurement via contractors rather than operators. |
| **Certification** | Class-society and operator qualification regimes. |
| **Buyer concentration** | Moderate, but the *decision* sits with survey contractors, not asset owners. |

**INFERRED — the most interesting commercial hypothesis in the case.** Offshore wind O&M is the one segment where the "one vehicle does survey *and* inspection" value proposition maps to a large, growing, non-government budget, and where the saving (a vessel day) is orders of magnitude larger than the component price. **There is no public evidence ARMADA is pursuing it.**

## Segment 5 — Allied defence organisations

Plausible follow-on to a US Navy transition (UK, Australia, Japan, Nordic navies all operate REMUS-class vehicles). **OBSERVED:** the sample shows Australia's C2 Robotics selling Speartooth LUUVs to the US Navy ($5.1M), evidencing a live allied market. **UNKNOWN:** export control (ITAR) exposure for a Navy-funded payload deployment system would need assessment. No evidence ARMADA is pursuing this.

## Summary — demonstrated versus potential

| Segment | Demonstrated relationship | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| **US Navy / DoD** | **Funded R&D customer** — Phase I, Phase I option, Phase II with exercised option and Mar 2026 incremental funding | Strong, but it is R&D funding, not product revenue |
| NSF | Completed STTR Phase I (2020–21) | Historical |
| NOAA | Completed SBIR Phase I (2024–25) | Historical, no follow-on |
| UUV OEMs | **None public** | — |
| Research institutions | **None public** | — |
| Offshore energy / survey | **None public** | — |
| Allied defence | **None public** | — |

**The commercial conclusion.** ARMADA has one demonstrated customer relationship: the US government, buying research and development. Across five years and roughly $3.0M of federal funding, **no commercial sale, pilot, LOI or named partner has been made public.** That may reflect a company sensibly focused on completing a Phase II — or it may reflect a company that has not yet tested commercial demand. Public evidence cannot distinguish these, and doing so is the first job of primary research.
