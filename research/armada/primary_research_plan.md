# ARMADA — Primary Research Plan

**Phase 3H** · **No one was contacted. No conversations occurred. No quotes exist.** This is the call plan we would run before an investment decision, in priority order.

Public information has taken this case as far as it can go. Six questions now gate the decision, and five of them are answerable in a single week of calls.

---

## Call 1 — Founders (Jeff Kaeli, CEO; Robin Littlefield, Lead Engineer)
**Hypothesis tested:** that ARMADA has begun a deliberate transition from contract R&D to a repeatable commercial product, and holds the IP rights to do it.

1. On the EPADS PCT (WO 2024/136933) you and WHOI are joint applicants. How is ownership allocated between you, and what field-of-use rights does ARMADA hold?
2. Has a US national-phase application been filed from that PCT? What is its status?
3. Do you hold any licence to WHOI's US 10,112,686 / 11,072,406 (payload deployment) or US 10,640,188 (passive ballast)? If not, does EPADS practise them?
4. Is the EPADS pod consumed on deployment — left on the seafloor with the payload — or recovered? *(This single answer determines whether the business has recurring unit economics.)*
5. What is the current Phase II status, and what specifically did the March 2026 CLIN 0004 funding buy?
6. What is your intended Phase III transition path, and is a programme office sponsoring it?
7. Has any UUV OEM integrated, tested, or committed to evaluate Asymmetric Propulsion?
8. What is the current headcount, and is Robin full-time at ARMADA or still at WHOI?
9. What has been raised in equity to date, from whom, and on what terms?
10. If you never raised venture capital, what would this company become?

**Increases conviction:** clear joint-ownership terms with commercialisation rights; pods are consumable; a named OEM in evaluation; a sponsoring programme office for Phase III.
**Decreases conviction:** EPADS rights unresolved or WHOI-controlled; pods recovered and reused; no OEM contact in five years; Phase III with no sponsor.

## Call 2 — WHOI technology transfer / licensing
**Hypothesis tested:** that ARMADA's IP position is durable and not structurally constrained by its licensor.

1. What do the two 2020/2025 exclusive licences cover — field of use, term, territory, sublicensing rights?
2. Are there diligence milestones or minimum royalties, and is ARMADA meeting them?
3. Under what conditions can the licences terminate or convert to non-exclusive?
4. How does WHOI treat jointly-applied IP with a spinout — is there a standard allocation?
5. WHOI itself sells vehicles and R&D to the Navy. How is the boundary with ARMADA managed?
6. ARMADA was described as the first spinout to use the WHOI Express License program. What does that programme standardise, and what does it leave open?
7. Does WHOI hold equity in ARMADA?

**Increases conviction:** broad field of use, sublicensing permitted, no near-term milestone risk, clear separation from WHOI's own commercial activity.
**Decreases conviction:** narrow field of use, unmet milestones, or WHOI competing directly in the same application.

---

## Call 3 — UUV OEM product or engineering lead (HII/REMUS, Teledyne, Kongsberg, or L3Harris)
**Hypothesis tested:** that an OEM would ever adopt a third-party propulsion architecture — the single largest commercial assumption in the case.

1. Walk me through what changes on your vehicle if propulsion moves from thruster-plus-fins to a single modulated blade. What has to be requalified?
2. How long is a realistic design-in cycle for a propulsion change, and who signs it off?
3. What reliability evidence would you require — hours, cycles, environments?
4. How much do you care about the acoustic signature of an unbalanced single-blade propeller?
5. Do you buy propulsion subsystems today, or build them?
6. Is "one vehicle does survey and inspection" a capability your customers are asking for?
7. Would you rather buy this, license it, or acquire the team?
8. Have you evaluated ARMADA?

**Increases conviction:** OEMs buy rather than build; the survey-plus-inspection pull is real; a plausible design-in path exists.
**Decreases conviction:** propulsion is treated as core IP built in-house; requalification cost is prohibitive; acoustic signature is disqualifying.

---

## Call 4 — Navy or former Navy undersea systems expert (programme office or NAWCAD-side)
**Hypothesis tested:** that EPADS has a credible route from Phase II to a programme of record.

1. What operational gap did the EPADS SBIR topic come from, and is that gap still funded?
2. How many Phase IIs from this contracting activity reach Phase III or a programme of record?
3. What placement accuracy would make this operationally useful versus merely interesting?
4. Is external payload carriage acceptable operationally, given a ≤25% mission-time penalty?
5. Who would own EPADS if it transitioned — a platform programme, or a payload programme?
6. Would the Navy prefer to buy this through a prime or directly from a small business?
7. What typically kills these transitions?

**Increases conviction:** a live, funded requirement with an identified transition sponsor.
**Decreases conviction:** the topic was exploratory, the gap has been closed another way, or transition requires a prime relationship ARMADA lacks.

---

## Call 5 — Independent underwater propulsion / vehicle dynamics expert
**Hypothesis tested:** that the technical claim survives contact with an unaffiliated specialist.

1. Reading claim 1 of US 9,873,499, how novel is intra-revolution speed modulation for thrust vectoring?
2. Where does control authority go at very low speed, when RPM is low? Is the station-keeping claim physically comfortable?
3. What are the vibration, bearing-life and fatigue implications of a single unbalanced blade over a long deployment?
4. What is the likely acoustic signature penalty versus a balanced multi-blade propeller?
5. What propulsive efficiency would you expect relative to a conventional arrangement?
6. What would you demand to see before believing the reliability claim?
7. Is the 2010 ACRBS prior art meaningfully different?

**Increases conviction:** the physics is sound and the low-speed case holds.
**Decreases conviction:** control authority collapses at low speed, or vibration/acoustics are fundamental rather than engineering problems.

---

## Call 6 — Offshore wind or subsea survey contractor
**Hypothesis tested:** that a large commercial market exists beyond defence — the thing that would make this venture-scale.

1. What does a day of subsea inspection cost you today, and what dominates that cost?
2. Do you deploy a separate ROV for close inspection after an AUV survey? What does that second mobilisation cost?
3. Would a single vehicle doing both change your operating model, or just your equipment list?
4. Who decides equipment — you, the asset owner, or the vehicle supplier?
5. What certification would a novel propulsion system need for your work?
6. Would you buy a vehicle with this, or expect your existing supplier to offer it?
7. Do you ever need to place instruments on the seafloor, and how do you do it today?

**Increases conviction:** the two-asset problem is real and expensive, and buyers can specify equipment.
**Decreases conviction:** vessel-day economics dominate everything and equipment choice sits with a conservative supplier.

---

## Call 7 — Research-institution AUV operator (not WHOI)
**Hypothesis tested:** that ARMADA can win reference customers outside its home institution.

1. Would you buy a propulsion upgrade for an existing vehicle, or only a whole new vehicle?
2. How do you evaluate new subsea hardware, and what is your risk tolerance for a three-person supplier?
3. Have you encountered ARMADA?
4. What would make you an early adopter?
5. How do you currently place instruments on the seafloor, and what does it cost in ship time?

**Increases conviction:** demand for retrofit; an accessible early-adopter path.
**Decreases conviction:** vehicles are bought whole and rarely modified; supplier-continuity risk is disqualifying.

---

## Call 8 — Former employee or close competitor *(only if one can be identified appropriately)*
**Hypothesis tested:** execution capacity and any material facts absent from public sources.

Questions on team depth beyond the three named principals; manufacturing readiness; how the Navy work has actually gone; and whether commercial development has been attempted.

---

## Sequencing

**Week 1:** Calls 1 and 2 — they resolve the IP question, which is the single largest binary in the case. If EPADS rights are structurally constrained, most of the rest is moot.
**Week 2:** Calls 3 and 4 — these test the two commercial routes (OEM design-in; Navy transition). Either can carry the case; neither is evidenced today.
**Week 3:** Calls 5, 6, 7 — technical validation and the size of the non-defence opportunity.
