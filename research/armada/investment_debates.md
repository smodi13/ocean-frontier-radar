# ARMADA — The Investment Debates

**Phase 3I** · The four debates below are the ones the evidence actually produces. Generic headings were discarded in favour of the questions that determine the answer.

---

## Debate 1 — Is ARMADA a component company or an engineering organisation?

**This is the debate. Everything else is subordinate to it.**

**CURRENT VIEW.** On public evidence, ARMADA today *is* an engineering organisation — a three-person team funded by sequential government development contracts — that holds an option on becoming a component company. Nothing public shows the option has been exercised.

**BULL EVIDENCE**
- $2,972,287 of non-dilutive federal funding across four agencies in five years, developing technology with no equity given up.
- The Navy **exercised an option** (Jan 2025) and **incrementally funded a new CLIN** (Mar 2026) — repeat customer decisions, not just an initial award.
- Two granted patents exclusively licensed, plus joint applicant status on the EPADS filing: a component company's asset base.
- EPADS pods, if consumed per deployment, would be a genuinely recurring unit business.

**BEAR EVIDENCE**
- **Five years, zero commercial revenue, zero named commercial customer, zero public OEM relationship.**
- The largest bucket in observed UUV procurement is R&D ($69.0M) — exactly where ARMADA earns. The company is well-adapted to the market it is in, which is not the market the venture case requires.
- Three publicly identified people. Manufacturing, qualification and enterprise sales capabilities are unevidenced.
- The March 2026 CLIN funding, while positive, extends the R&D-contract model rather than diversifying from it.

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW.** Whether commercial development has been attempted and failed, deferred deliberately until Phase II completes, or never started. Public sources cannot distinguish these and the three imply very different companies.

**UPGRADE CONDITION.** A named OEM in active evaluation, or a first commercial purchase order, or confirmation that pods are consumable with a credible volume forecast.
**DOWNGRADE CONDITION.** Confirmation that no commercial customer conversation has occurred in five years, or that the team intends to remain SBIR-funded.
**PRIMARY RESEARCH.** Calls 1 (founders), 3 (OEM), 6 (offshore contractor).

## Debate 2 — Does ARMADA control the IP behind its best-evidenced product?

**CURRENT VIEW.** Materially better than Phase 2.5 concluded, and still not resolved. ARMADA is a **joint applicant and joint listed assignee** with WHOI on the EPADS PCT (WO 2024/136933), with its CEO and Lead Engineer among the named inventors. That is a real ownership interest, not a licence gap. But the terms are invisible and no US grant is evidenced.

**BULL EVIDENCE**
- Joint applicant/assignee status on the EPADS filing — ARMADA has a direct interest, not merely permission.
- The claimed mechanism (valve-flooded cavity, zero net buoyancy) is **distinct** from WHOI's older vacuum-hold patents, consistent with genuinely new invention arising from the Navy work.
- Priority date (Sep 2022) sits inside the funded programme.
- Two propulsion patents are exclusively licensed with an announced, specific field of use.

**BEAR EVIDENCE**
- **No US national-phase application from the PCT could be found.** A PCT publication confers no US exclusionary right by itself; without a US grant there may be nothing enforceable in the primary market.
- Ownership split, field of use, sublicensing and commercialisation rights between the co-applicants are entirely unknown.
- No licence to WHOI's US 10,112,686 / 11,072,406 (payload deployment) or US 10,640,188 (passive ballast) is publicly announced, and the persistent-sensing concept plausibly practises the last of these.
- ARMADA's ability to **sublicense** the propulsion patents is unknown, which constrains a licensing business model.

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW.** Essentially all of the commercial terms. This is invisible from outside and cheap to resolve from inside.

**UPGRADE CONDITION.** A filed/published US national-phase application, plus confirmation ARMADA holds commercialisation rights with a broad field of use.
**DOWNGRADE CONDITION.** No US filing and no plan to file; or WHOI holding effective control of commercialisation decisions.
**PRIMARY RESEARCH.** Calls 1 and 2. **This is the first call to make** — it is binary, cheap to answer, and gates the rest.

## Debate 3 — Will any OEM actually replace a qualified propulsion architecture?

**CURRENT VIEW.** This is the least-evidenced and most load-bearing assumption in the propulsion product line, and our current lean is sceptical.

**BULL EVIDENCE**
- The underlying pain is real: every fin actuator is a pressure penetration, a failure mode and a drag source.
- The economic argument is not about component price — it is about avoiding a second asset and a second vessel mobilisation, which is worth orders of magnitude more.
- Two granted patents and peer-reviewed conference publications (2018, 2019) give a credible technical foundation.
- A self-reported 3D steering test passed in Sep 2022.

**BEAR EVIDENCE**
- **Changing propulsion invalidates vehicle qualification.** The buyer of a UUV is buying a qualified system; this is a design decision with multi-year consequences, not a purchase.
- Every target OEM is also the party best able to build it in-house, and the claim covers regulating *motor speed* — alternative means such as cyclic pitch may sit outside it.
- **No public performance data of any kind** for the product ARMADA says it is "bringing to market": no efficiency, turning radius, power draw or acoustic figures.
- **INFERRED physical concern:** control authority from intra-revolution modulation should scale with RPM, so it may be weakest at the very low speeds the station-keeping pitch depends on.
- Single-blade propulsion has prior art back to 1945; the concept space is not empty.

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW.** Whether any OEM has ever evaluated it. Whether low-speed control authority is adequate. Acoustic signature.

**UPGRADE CONDITION.** Published or shared quantitative comparison against a finned baseline; a named OEM evaluation.
**DOWNGRADE CONDITION.** OEMs confirm propulsion is core in-house IP; or an independent expert judges low-speed control authority insufficient.
**PRIMARY RESEARCH.** Calls 3 and 5.

## Debate 4 — Can the observable market support a venture outcome?

**CURRENT VIEW.** Not on the federal procurement visible in our sample. The venture case requires markets we cannot see.

**BULL EVIDENCE**
- The sample is demonstrably incomplete: large programmes of record and classified work use nomenclature our keywords miss, so true undersea spending is far larger.
- The payload-deployment bucket is small **because the capability does not exist yet** — you cannot observe a market before the product. This is a fair rebuttal to a bottom-up critique of genuinely new capability.
- Complete-platform procurement of $60.3M in the sample becomes addressable the moment an OEM designs the subsystem in.
- Offshore wind O&M is a large, growing, non-government budget where the "one asset not two" argument is worth a vessel day, and it is entirely absent from this analysis because ARMADA shows no sign of pursuing it.

**BEAR EVIDENCE**
- **Narrow addressable observed procurement: ~$826K/year.** Broad adjacency: ~$6.3M/year. A $30M revenue threshold is roughly **36×** the narrow figure and **5×** the broad figure.
- Thirteen years of data show no growth trend in the component/spares bucket.
- The most frequent supplier in the sample is a **distributor** (W S Darley, 24 appearances), consistent with a market that buys commodity hardware and complete systems, not novel subsystems.
- The scenario model reaches only $22.1M at Base — short of the $30M threshold — and only by assuming 600 pods/year and 120 propulsion modules/year, both entirely unevidenced.

**WHAT WE DON'T KNOW.** Pod consumption economics; whether a commercial (non-defence) channel is being pursued at all; the size of allied/export demand.

**UPGRADE CONDITION.** Evidence of a commercial offshore channel, or pod volumes credibly in the hundreds per year.
**DOWNGRADE CONDITION.** Pods recovered rather than consumed, and no commercial channel in development.
**PRIMARY RESEARCH.** Calls 1, 4, 6.

---

## How the debates interact

Debate 2 is **binary and cheap** — resolve it first; a bad answer ends the process.
Debates 1 and 4 are **the same question seen from inside and outside** the company: is there a repeatable product, and is there enough market for one? Both hinge on the EPADS consumable-pod question, which is a single sentence from a founder.
Debate 3 governs the *second* product line and is the slower, harder one. It could be wrong and the case could still work on EPADS alone — but not the reverse.
