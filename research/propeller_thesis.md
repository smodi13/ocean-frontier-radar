### Investment philosophy, in their words

- They look for teams and technologies that "cross-cut sectors and have an ocean-sized impact on the world," and describe the founders they want as pursuing something "crazy-ambitious." ([At The Ocean's Edge](https://propellervc.com/blog/at-the-oceans-edge))

### The "ocean's edge" framing — why it matters for sourcing

This is the single most important framing device for this project. Propeller explicitly rejects a narrow "ocean tech" category. In [At The Ocean's Edge](https://propellervc.com/blog/at-the-oceans-edge) (Reece Pacheco, 8 Jun 2026) they ask: *"What are the ocean's true edges? What are the ways in which the ocean is affecting and affected by our greatest industries?"*

The corollary is stated even more directly in [The Climate Market Map](https://propellervc.com/blog/the-climate-market-map) (Eliza Harrison, 24 Jun 2025), where Propeller declines to treat the ocean as a standalone sector and instead treats it as **"a cross-sector catalyst for climate innovation"**, mapping ocean innovation across all seven CTVC climate sectors (Energy, Transportation, Food & Ag, Industry, Built Environment, Carbon, Climate Risk).

**[Inferred]** For sourcing purposes this means a keyword filter on "ocean" is the *wrong* primary filter. Several portfolio companies would not surface from an ocean keyword search at all — Allium Engineering (stainless-clad rebar), Vema Hydrogen (geologic hydrogen), Blue Energy (SMRs), Nexxa.ai (industrial software agents), Rewind (terrestrial biomass to the Black Sea). The operative test is closer to *"is the ocean the enabling context, the customer environment, or the physical mechanism?"* — a test that has to be applied to the technology, not matched against a word. This directly shapes the prioritization framework in `prioritization_framework.md`.

## 2. Public Investment Themes

Propeller publicly organizes around **three themes**. These are their words, taken from [propellervc.com](https://propellervc.com/):

### Industrials
> "Energy, shipping, transportation, automation, robotics, surveying, inspection, insurance, materials, and adaptation/dual-use applications"

### Carbon
> "Emissions reduction/removal technologies plus sensing, monitoring, and modeling capabilities"

### Organics
> "Innovation in food, feed, fertilizer, and biologic value creation for aquaculture and agriculture"

Propeller has said the themes are organizational rather than rigid: their thesis "is organized into three investment themes: Carbon, Organics, and Industrials" ([Surfacing Propeller's Portfolio](https://propellervc.com/blog/announcing-portfolio)).

### Theme drift over time — **Observed**

The 2022 launch language was narrower and more explicitly climate-framed: "Ocean Carbon (CO2 removal, blue carbon tech), Ocean Organics (marine-based energy/pharmaceuticals), and Ocean Industrials (decarbonizing maritime industries like shipping and offshore wind)" ([Launch PR](https://propellervc.com/blog/launchpressrelease), Oct 2022).

The current site language is broader — Industrials now explicitly names **automation, robotics, surveying, inspection, insurance, materials, and adaptation/dual-use**. That is a meaningfully wider aperture than "decarbonizing maritime industries."

**[Inferred]** The firm has widened from "ocean-climate" toward "ocean-industrial + climate + dual-use." Three pieces of public evidence support this reading:

1. **Dual-use.** [Why Ocean Innovation Is Often Dual-Use](https://propellervc.com/blog/why-ocean-innovation-is-often-dual-use) (17 Apr 2026) states plainly: *"Propeller is not pivoting to defense-tech. But…we have a bunch of dual-use companies in our growing portfolio already."* It names Indeximate, Orpheus Ocean, Vatn Systems, Navier, Fleet Robotics, Fleetzero, and Ness Sea, and reports a strong Navy demand signal.
2. **Adaptation.** [Can the Ocean Still Beat the Heat?](https://propellervc.com/blog/can-the-ocean-still-beat-the-heat) (Steven Fox, 13 Aug 2026 — eight days before this dossier) argues for adaptation "pain-killers" alongside mitigation, and explicitly names materials engineering (hull coatings, lubricants, buoyancy foams), cooling technology, aquaculture temperature management, and HAB/pathogen monitoring as areas of interest. Closing line: *"The question isn't whether the pain arrives at the coast, it's who's building the pain-killers when it does."*
3. **Compute at sea.** [Summer Slowdown? The Ocean Is Heating Up](https://propellervc.com/blog/summer-slowdown-the-ocean-is-heating) (Reece Pacheco, 28 Jul 2026) flags ocean-sited data centers, citing portfolio companies Aikido and Blue Energy, and notes YC's "compute at sea" call. It also names the hard problems: subsea interconnect and cabling, corrosion and materials science in saltwater, and regulatory frameworks for ocean use and heat discharge.

**This is the most sourcing-relevant fact in the dossier.** Propeller's two most recent posts before the analyst hiring announcement point at *coastal/marine heat adaptation* and *ocean-sited compute*, and both resolve into the same underlying technical bottlenecks: **marine materials, corrosion, biofouling, thermal management, and subsea power/data infrastructure.**

## 4. Investment Process

From [How We Invest](https://propellervc.com/blog/how-we-invest) (updated 6 Jul 2026) — **Observed**:

1. **Origination.** A partner identifies an opportunity.
2. **Internal socialization.** That partner builds internal support by bringing in **at least one colleague**. This is an explicit gate before diligence.
3. **Diligence.** The firm requests documentation, "ideally a data room," and prepares IC materials.
4. **Investment Committee.** "All deals go through our IC," which consists of **five investment partners**. The IC approves, requests more information, or declines.
5. **Conviction standard.** "To invest, we must have conviction."

They also note: many portfolio companies "initially secure grants for research before launching their startup to commercialize innovations" ([How We Invest](https://propellervc.com/blog/how-we-invest)).

> **This single sentence is the strongest possible endorsement of the Ocean Frontier Radar premise.** Propeller states as an observed pattern that its companies typically pass through a *research-grant stage before company formation*. A sourcing system built on grant and publication data is therefore watching the exact stage Propeller says its companies come from — not a proxy for it.

They are explicit that a pass is often a model-fit issue rather than a quality judgment: sometimes "the investment opportunity just isn't a great fit for our model." They encourage idea-stage founders to connect early.

**[Unknown]:** decision timelines, ownership targets, reserve ratios, whether IC is unanimous or majority, and how the WHOI/university partnerships feed the IC process. None of this is public.

## 5. Sourcing Philosophy

Propeller's sourcing is unusually well documented for a fund of its size, and it is **institutional, not opportunistic**.

### 5.1 Formal research-institution partnerships — **Observed**

- **Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI)** — founding partner, announced with the fund in Oct 2022. The partnership is described as providing "access to the brightest minds and intellectual property (IP) in ocean science and innovation." WHOI President Peter de Menocal is quoted in the launch release. ([Launch PR](https://propellervc.com/blog/launchpressrelease); [WHOI press room](https://www.whoi.edu/press-room/news-release/propeller-announces-100-million-fund-to-invest-in-ocean-climate-companies))
- **Four additional institutions added 13 Nov 2023** — Oregon State University, University of Hawai'i at Mānoa, UC San Diego / **Scripps Institution of Oceanography**, and University of Rhode Island **Graduate School of Oceanography**. The stated purpose is identifying commercializable opportunities from ocean science research, with tech transfer explicitly named. Quotes from Margaret Leinen (UCSD), Paula Bontempi (URI GSO Dean), and Julie Pullen (Propeller Chief Scientist). ([URI GSO announcement](https://web.uri.edu/gso/news/propeller-welcomes-new-partnerships-with-oregon-state-university-university-of-hawaii-university-of-california-san-diego-and-university-of-rhode-island/))

**These five institutions are not a guess about where to look. They are Propeller's declared sourcing surface.** They anchor `sourcing_universe.md`.

### 5.2 Ocean MBA — a founder-formation funnel they own — **Observed**

Propeller runs an **Ocean MBA**: a short intensive program for ocean-sector founders from idea to seed stage. The first WHOI cohort (24–25 Jan 2024) drew **30+ WHOI researchers, engineers, staff and students, plus scientists from Bigelow Laboratory for Ocean Sciences**, and covered demystifying startups/VC for academics, customer discovery, IP strategy, and pitch development. Faculty included Rick Murray (WHOI Deputy Director), Blaine Grimes (Chief Ventures Officer, GMRI), and Matt Eisaman (Ebb Carbon). ([Making Waves at WHOI](https://propellervc.com/blog/making-waves-at-whoi-with-the-ocean-mba)) Multiple cohorts have run since ([2024 Ocean MBA](https://propellervc.com/blog/2024-ocean-mba), [Ocean MBA 2025](https://propellervc.com/blog/oceanmba-2025), [our third Ocean MBA](https://propellervc.com/blog/our-third-ocean-mba-fast-focused)).

At least two portfolio companies — **CarbonRun** and **Hum.ai** — came through it.

**[Inferred]** The Ocean MBA is a deliberate mechanism for converting *scientists* into *founders* early enough that Propeller sees them pre-formation. It is upstream of a normal deal funnel.

### 5.3 Hackathons with research institutions and industry — **Observed**

- **Ocean CDR Hackathon at WHOI** (2023), with speakers from Ebb Carbon, Kelson Marine, and Planetary Technologies. ([Ocean CDR hackathon](https://propellervc.com/blog/ocean_cdr_hackathon))
- **Offshore Wind Hackathon at WHOI with Ørsted** (2023), on offshore-wind biodiversity — featuring WHOI scientists Anthony Kirincich (wind forecasting), Heidi Sosik (ecology), and Annette Govindarajan (**environmental DNA sampling**). ([Ørsted hackathon](https://propellervc.com/blog/propeller-and-%C3%B8rsted-host-offshore-wind-hackathon-at-whoi))

### 5.4 Ecosystem and market-building — **Observed**

Propeller joined **Canada's Ocean Supercluster** to launch a **Market Solutions Platform** alongside Katapult Ocean, S2G Investments, Stanford Doerr School of Sustainability, Economist Impact, and Fisheries and Oceans Canada — focused on market development for ocean climate solutions. ([Advancing Markets for Ocean Climate Solutions](https://propellervc.com/blog/advancing-markets-for-ocean-climate-solutions))

**[Inferred]** This explains the notable Nova Scotia density in the portfolio (CarbonRun, pHathom) — Canada's ocean ecosystem is a live channel, not a coincidence.

### 5.5 Conferences and convenings — **Observed**

Propeller writes about and hosts events at: SF Climate Week ([maritime autonomy panel](https://propellervc.com/blog/what-maritime-autonomy-looks-like-in-motion), [sfcw2025](https://propellervc.com/blog/sfcw2025)), NY Climate Week ([nycw-surf-adventure](https://propellervc.com/blog/nycw-surf-adventure)), an [NYC OceanTech Summit](https://propellervc.com/blog/nyc-oceantech-summit), the [World Ocean Summit](https://propellervc.com/blog/world-ocean-summit), and COP ([COP28](https://propellervc.com/blog/cop28), [COP30](https://propellervc.com/blog/cop30)). A US Navy event is referenced in the dual-use post.

### 5.6 Other visible channels — **Observed**

LP introductions (Navier), operating partners (Indeximate), personal network (VATN), and an Operating Partner spinning out as a founder (Orpheus Ocean).

### 5.7 What this means for Ocean Frontier Radar — **[Inferred]**

Propeller's sourcing strength is *relationship density at five specific oceanographic institutions plus a founder-formation program they operate themselves.* Its structural limitation is the same thing: those channels are deep but narrow, and they are largely **US coastal oceanography + Canadian ocean ecosystem**.

A defensible outside-in contribution is therefore **not** "find ocean startups" — Propeller's network does that better. It is:

1. **Breadth across institutions they have no formal partnership with** (Scripps and OSU are partners; Georgia Tech, Michigan, Maine, Delaware, Texas A&M, Florida Atlantic, Stevens, WHOI-adjacent Bigelow are not).
2. **Breadth across funders** — NSF SBIR/STTR, NOAA, ARPA-E, DOE, ONR/Navy SBIR — where commercialization intent is explicit in the award itself.
3. **Adjacent-industry technologies where the ocean is the *deployment environment* rather than the research topic** — corrosion, coatings, thermal management, power electronics, materials. These will never appear in an "ocean tech" list, but Allium and Vema show Propeller buys them.

I make no claim about what is or is not already in Propeller's pipeline. This is framed strictly as an outside-in sourcing objective.

## 6. The Analyst Role — what the system must demonstrate

From [We're hiring our first analyst](https://propellervc.com/blog/were-hiring-our-first-analyst) (10 Aug 2026) — **Observed**:

- **Location / comp:** Boston, MA, in-person/hybrid. $120,000 base plus performance bonus.
- **Core work:** "lead early technical and commercial diligence: pressure-test the science and engineering, map the competitive landscape"; founder calls; financial models; investment memos; sector theses and research.
- **Tooling, explicitly:** "sourcing scrapers, diligence dashboards, AI-assisted research workflows."
- **Background:** 1–3 years post-undergrad in engineering, hard-tech startups, or VC; "a degree or serious hands-on background in engineering, the hard sciences, or computer science"; ability to read technical papers and communicate clearly.
- **Disposition:** "high-agency and low-ego."
- **Preferred:** exposure to energy, maritime, materials, carbon removal, robotics, or applied AI; and evidence you have "shipped something real — a product, a research result, a company, an open-source project."
- **Process note:** applicants are asked to share work samples **and to disclose any AI tool usage.**

**[Inferred] — the two design constraints this places on Ocean Frontier Radar:**

1. The listing names *sourcing scrapers, diligence dashboards, AI-assisted research workflows* as things the analyst builds. This project should be a real instance of all three, not a mock-up of one.
2. The AI-disclosure requirement means the system's provenance discipline is itself part of the work sample. A tool that cleanly separates **source fact** from **AI inference** — the core of `evidence_model.md` — is responsive to something they explicitly asked about.
